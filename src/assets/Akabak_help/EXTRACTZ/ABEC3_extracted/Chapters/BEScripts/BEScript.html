Solving-scripts are used for the Boundary Element Analysis only.

General properties are discussed in chapter Scripts and in chapter Syntax.

Solving-scripts need to be registered with the Project, see Add Solving Script. Registered scripts are monitored and re-loaded automatically if out-of-date, see Updating Files.


The purpose of the solving-script is to provide basically five things:

  1. Global properties
  2. Controlling the solver
  3. Boundary geometry
  4. Boundary conditions
  5. Driving

Global Properties

Global properties set the engine into a certain state, which affects the interpretation of sub-sequent entries and calculations. Examples are: Dimension, symmetry, speed of sound, element-types, etc. There are three sections, which can control global properties:

  • Control_Solver
  • Subdomain_Properties
  • MeshFile_Properties
  • Formula

Controlling the Solver

Control_Solver is also the section to provide settings for the solver of the Helmholtz Integral Equation. Of course, we need to provide the frequency range but also details on the kind and size of the finite elements as explained in chapter Meshing.

Boundary Geometry

Most of the script is dedicated to the specification of the geometry of the boundaries. There are three ways to input boundaries:

  • Node and Elements
  • Mesh-files
  • Element factories (Diaphragm and Shell)

Specifications can be parameterized, see section Formula and chapter Parameterization of Scripts.

Boundary Conditions

By default all boundaries provide Neumann boundary conditions, which means that the normal of the field-velocity at the boundary is zero or equal to the driving motion of the surface.

Optionally ABEC provides impedance boundary conditions, which relate the surface pressure and the normal velocity at the boundary. See section WallImpedance for specification.

Driving

The sound-field can be driven in two ways:

  • Boundary motion (Driving and Diaphragm)
  • Pressure points (Pressure_Points or Pressure_Box)

As explained in chapter Driving Groups ABEC provides late-linking of actual driving-values. This means you can alter driving values at observation-stage without the need of re-solving.

Boundary motion is a special boundary condition where the normal component of field-velocity matches the motion of the boundary element. This can be a loudspeaker diaphragm but also a virtual diaphragm such as the driving throat cross-section of a waveguide. Using the Driving-section you can make any Element a vibrating surface. Elements, which are produced by Diaphragm are always driven.

Pressure_Points form a different category of driving as Pressure_Points are very abstract from the start. A pressure point forms an ideal point source of pressure. Pressure_Points most frequently are used in the analysis of rooms, where they represent the sound sources in the room. Pressure_Points do not fully couple to Lumped Element Networks.

Pressure_Box elements are similar to Pressure_Points. Only that the acoustic pressure can have direction, which is graphically symbolized with the help of a box.
