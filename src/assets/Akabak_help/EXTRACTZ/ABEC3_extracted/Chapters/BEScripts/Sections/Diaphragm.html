Diaphragm works as an element-factory, which conveniently creates boundary elements for a variety of shapes: From a very simple disc to a complicated oval-cone loudspeaker including magnet. Diaphragm comprises properties of what was said at Elements- and Driving-section. For Dim=CircSym only circular shapes are making sense.

The Diaphragm is practical but has its limitations. In case you need a more detailed analysis of your driver it may be better to use CAD + mesh-files.

The shape of the diaphragm is simply controlled by the way dimension-parameters are specified as explained in Diaphragm Shapes.

HasBaffle=true (default) creates a rectangular baffle around the circular, elliptical or oval diaphragm, which size is identical to the outer-diameters of the Diaphragm.

Because often, the rear of the diaphragm needs to be modeled as well, a Diaphragm can inherit parameters from another Diaphragm referenced by RefDiaphragm=. Of course, this feature is also practical if you implement multiple copies of the same Diaphragm.

The rigid body motion of the moving part of the diaphragm is assumed to be in direction of the normal of the disc or the main-axis of the loudspeaker cone. Currently other motions and higher mechanical modes are ignored.


If Simple=true is specified then a simpler shape is produced by the diaphragm-factory, which often is sufficient especially at low frequencies.



Example

With the help of a Diaphragm you can create very complex shapes or make the most basic scripts, as for example a dome in an Infinite Baffle:

  Control_Solver
    f1=10;  f2=1000;  NumFrequencies=20;  Abscissa=log
    MeshFrequency=3kHz
    Meshing=Delaunay

  Subdomain_Properties
    ELType=Exterior
    IBPlane=z

  Diaphragm  "D1"
    DrvGroup=1001
    HasBaffle=false
    dD=120mm;  hD1=10mm;
