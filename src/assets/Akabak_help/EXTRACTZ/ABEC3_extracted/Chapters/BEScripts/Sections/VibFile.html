Section VibFile is used in the solving script of the Boundary Element Analysis. VibFile creates boundary elements automatically from a special file with geometric and vibrational data. This file is referenced with the help of parameter VibFileAlias=.

Currently, VibFile supports files, which are exported by the Klippel laser-scanner software.

The vibrational data should be available for the frequency range of analysis. Outside the range, ABEC substitutes vibration-values of the nearest available frequency point. Within the range, vibration values are interpolated.

Example

  VibFile "Vib"
    SubDomain=1
    VibFileAlias=Vib
    DrvGroup=1001
    NoBaffle
    NormType=PosPolar 12.8mm 0deg

ABEC assumes a static quasi-stationary velocity profile. Static means that the velocity distribution is not changed in frequency, amplitude and spatially by the acoustic load. In other words, the imported velocity profile is the same for a speaker build in a waveguide or free radiation. Even, if you change the acoustic properties, say to water, it will still be the same distribution. In reality, of course, the vibration of a body will alter with changing acoustic radiation impedance. This is especially true for a dense medium such as water. However, in air, this alteration may be small enough to yield a good approximation, hopefully better than to assume a piston movement.

The coupling to the lumped element network is via the radiation-impedance, which in turn is based on the acoustic power on the diaphragm. If a diaphragm has a velocity-distribution then this is taken into account during integrations. For details, see [9].

Modes of Operation

By bringing together mechanical vibrational data and the acoustic simulation, naturally, there are several modes of operation:

Case Vibrational data Acoustic simulation Symmetry UseLineScan= Results in
1) Full 3D scan Dim=3D No symmetry false 3D-scan
2) Full 3D scan Dim=3D Sym=any sym false Line-scan
3) Full 3D scan Dim=CircSym - false Line-scan
4) Full 3D scan Dim=all modes Sym=all sym true Line-scan
5) Line-scan Dim=all modes Sym=all sym - Line-scan

During import of a 3D-vibrational data-set ABEC directly creates both, a 3D representation and a line-scan. The latter is created by forming a mean-value in circumferential direction of the vibrational data. The mean-forming is performed complex-valued so that symmetric modes cancel by interference.

As the above table demonstrates, only case 1) results in the application of the original 3D data-set. In any other case ABEC makes use of the circumferential mean-value (line-scan), automatically.

With the help of parameter UseLineScan= you can enforce the application of the line-scan mode.

Some Notes

Note, that VibFiles yield an approximation, albeit in most cases a better one than assuming piston type vibration. Because of this there are limits of accuracy, which is to say that more data, or a higher resolution, may not yield better results in all cases.

For example during measurement of circular structures circumferential modes may not be excited, albeit potentially they are ready to spring into action. Under certain circumstances acoustic forces can excite these kind of modes. Because ABEC does not perform a full vibro-acoustic coupling this effect cannot be taken into account.

Try to make the vibrational scan a course as possible. For example, the Klippel scanner software can help you to identify dominant radiating modes (although this depends very much on the radiation condition).

For example, for low frequency analysis we may need only a very course scan. Detailed scans may yield superb simulation results at higher frequencies, however on the cost of calculation time.
