Constant value of acoustic resistance of component AcouResistance.

The unit is [Ra] = Pa·s/m3. In the script the unit is specified as "Pas/m3", for example

    Ra=10Pas/m3

Viscosity and Thermal Exchange [15]

In many cases the resistance is caused by viscosity where adjacent layers of gas molecules with different velocity are rubbing against one another. Directly at the walls, the molecules are at rest with the wall. Maximum movement occurs in the center. In between molecule-layers are moving differently and causing friction, which is greatest close to the walls and decreases rapidly towards the center.

The above scenario yields viscosity and thermal exchange. Approximately, the frequency dependency of the resistance increases with Ra ∝ √f:

η dyn. viscosity [η] = Pa·s, η = 18.2·10-6 Pa·s (air @ T = 20°C)

dh Thermal boundary layer dh = √(2·K/(ρ·ω·cp)), [dh] = m

K thermal conductivity [K] = W/(m·K), because K ≈ 5/3·η·cv, dh = √(10·η/(3·γ·ρ·ω)), dh = 2.4·10-6/√f (air @ T = 20° C).

The specific resistance Rs has to be divided by the effective area S, in order to obtain the acoustic resistance: Ra = Rs/S.

For example, the wire-mesh, which may cover the tunnel of a vented loudspeaker cabinet provides an acoustic resistance. For a mesh-density of 120 wires per inch and 0.092 mm gauge the table above gives a specific resistance of Rs = 13.5 Pa·s/m. The vent diameter shall be dD = 100mm then the cross-sectional area is thus: SD = π·dD2/4. The acoustic resistance is then:

Ra = Rs/SD = 13.5 Pa·s/m /0.00785 m2 = 1719 Pa·s/m3

On the other hand, the acoustic resistance due to the viscosity in this vent is approximately Ra = 120Pa·s/m3 (assuming a length of L = 200 mm and a frequency of 100Hz).

Thus the resistance of the wire-mesh is more than ten times higher than the resistance caused by viscosity inside the tunnel.

  AcouResistance  'Ra1'
    Node=5=6
    Ra=1719Pas/m3
