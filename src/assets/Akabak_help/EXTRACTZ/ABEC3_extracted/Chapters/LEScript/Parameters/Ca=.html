Identifier Ca=
Units m3/Pa
Format Floating point number
Purpose Acoustic compliance
Used in AcouCompliance

Constant value of acoustic compliance of component AcouCompliance.

The value of Ca is a constant with the dimension [Ca] = m3/Pa. It is the inverse of a spring-stiffness. In the script the unit is specified as "m3/Pa", for example

    Ca=1.23e-9m3/Pa

Derivation

The acoustic compliance is derived from a duct, which is closed at one end so that the velocity of the air-particles is zero at the closing wall. In the frequency range in which the diameter of the duct is in the range from approximately 0.1/√f < dD < 20/f, the acoustic impedance of such a duct is:

Za = -jρc/SD·cot(k·L)

with air-density ρ, speed of sound c, cross-sectional area of the duct SD = π·dD2/4, wave-number k = ω/c and length of the duct L. For small values of k·L, the cotangent can be approximated by the first of the two terms of the series:

cot(k·L) = 1/(k·L) - 1/3·k·L - 1/45·(k·L)3 - ...

The acoustic impedance Za then becomes approximately:

with volume of the closed duct Vb, acoustic compliance Ca, acoustic mass Ma'. This approximation applies for closed ducts with a length approximately L < λ/7. The acoustic compliance Ca is accordingly:

Ca = Vb/(ρ·c2)

Series Mass

The acoustic mass Ma' is in series with Ca and is accordingly:

Ma' = 1/3·L·ρ/SD

Note, that this series-mass Ma’ is 1/3rd of the acoustic mass of an open duct (see AcouMass). Using the above approximation the equivalent circuit-diagram of the low-frequency approximated closed duct is:


Note, that Ma' stems from the lumped element derivation of the duct. Ma' is always present but may vary in value depending on the actual shape and size of the cavity. The AcouCompliance element is not intended to simulate acoustic ducts, therefore Ma' is not part of the AcouCompliance model. For higher frequencies the components Duct and Enclosure are better suited, both of which make use of the formula using the cotangent- or tangent-function.

The above curves compare the reactance of a compliance to a closed duct. At low frequencies the compliance serves as a good approximation. At higher frequencies the simple lumped element does not show the resonances of the duct.


Examples

1) Example. At node 5 there is an acoustic compliance, which volume corresponds to Vb=2mm3. The value is calculated with the help of the inline-formula.

AcouCompliance  'Ca1'
    Node=5
    Ca={ Vb=2e-9; roh=1.2; c=344;  Vb/(roh*sqr(c)) }

2) Example. Similar to the first example, except that Ca is entered directly here.

AcouCompliance  'Ca1'
    Node=5
    Ca=14.084e-15m3/Pa

Damping Material

If the volume of the acoustical compliance is filled with a light porous material, the acoustic compliance Ca is often found to have increased by some percent. What is the reason for this?

If a gas is compressed very rapidly, its local temperature increases and, vice versa, when it relaxes, its temperature is reduced. For frequencies in the audio range, the air compression is adiabatically:

p·Vbχ = constant

with adiabatic exponent χ = 1.4 (for air). The equation for the compliance can also be written as:

Ca = Vb/(χ·p0)

with static air-pressure p0 = 101.33 kPa at zero altitude and T = 20°C.

If the volume Vb is filled uniformly with a material, which compensates for the rapid temperature changes of the sound wave by absorbing and re-emitting the heat, the changes of state would be more or less isothermal. The adiabatic exponent is then reduced to χ = 1.0 and the compliance is larger.

Damping material has further effects. Beside changing the thermal transfer the added material also increases damping and the effective acoustical mass.
