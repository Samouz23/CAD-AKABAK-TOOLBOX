Identifier Ma=
Units kg/m4
Format Floating point number
Purpose Acoustic mass
Used in AcouMass

Constant value of acoustic mass of component AcouMass.

The value of Ma is a constant with the dimension [Ma] = kg/m4. In the script the unit is specified as "kg/m4", for example

    Ma=0.5kg/m4

Derivation

The acoustic mass is derived from an open duct (p = 0). Its dimensions are assumed to be significantly smaller than the wavelength. In the frequency range in which the diameter of the duct is in the range 0.1/√f < dD < 20/f, the acoustic impedance of an open duct is:

Za = jρc/SD·tan(k·L)

with air-density ρ, speed of sound c, cross-sectional area of the duct SD = π·dD2/4, wave-number k = ω/c and length of the duct L.

At very low frequencies, only the first term of the Taylor-expansion of the tan-function may be used and Za becomes Za = jω·Ma, where Ma is the acoustic mass:

Ma = M/SD2 = ρ·L/SD

with air-mass M in the duct.

End Correction

In many cases, the acoustic mass of the duct is somewhat greater because of the radiation-impedance at both ends of the duct. The imaginary part forms an air-load, which equivalent acoustic mass Ma' should be added to Ma. At very low frequencies below fD = c/(π·dD) the air-load for one side is approximately:

Ma' = 0.54·ρ/dD = 0.65/dD

 The duct terminates at a large baffle.

Ma' = 0.39·ρ/dD = 0.5/dD

 The duct terminates freely.

Ma' should then be included for each side. The procedure is called end-correction. In extreme cases the acoustic mass may consist only, or predominantly, of the end-correction, for example in the case of a simple hole in a thin wall. A good value for Ma' can also be obtained by measuring the radiation impedance provided by the Boundary Element Simulation.


With increasing frequency, the wavelength gets smaller and the given equations for Ma yield a large error. In this case it is better to replace the AcouMass by the Duct element or by a Boundary Element Model.

Acoustic masses occur not only in passages and ducts, but also in duct systems if the cross-section changes or obstructions are present. Also holes of a mesh and of damping material induces acoustic mass. Finally any vibrating material may be expressed as an equivalent acoustic mass.

The following formulae are approximations for the frequency range in which the wavelength is large. If, for example, you need a value of the effect of a constriction, a bend, etc. These examples may form a suitable base for an estimation.


Duct with Constriction

Two ducts with identical cross-section have between them a thin wall with a hole of diameter dL. Let the area of the hole be much smaller than the cross-sectional area of the duct. The dimensions of the duct are also much smaller than the wavelength.



According to [15] the acoustic mass is given by

Ma = ρ/dL

with air-density ρ. Thus, with ρ=1.2kg/m3 and dL=20mm this results in Ma=60kg/m4. In the equivalent circuit-diagram, Ma is in series with the output of the first duct and the input of the second.

  AcouMass  'Ma1'
    Node=4=5
    Ma=60kg/m4

Duct with 90°-Bent


The above figure shows a 90° bend in the duct-path. If the cross-section of the ducts do not change, then an approximate value for the acoustic mass is given by [15]:

Ma ≈ 1.85/HD

Where HD is the height of the rectangular duct in meters. The mass is dependent only on the cross-section in the plane of the bend. The acoustic mass increases with the sharpness of the bend. In addition there is an acoustic resistance and a compliance.
