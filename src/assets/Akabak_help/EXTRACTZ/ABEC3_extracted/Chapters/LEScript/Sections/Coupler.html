The Coupler is a special component, which implements an ideal transformer into the network. Ideal transformers are used in modeling coupling of the mechanical to the acoustical domain by means of a diaphragm, or generally, in equivalent networks. For example see Electro-Dyn Components.

Nodes

The terminals of the primary side are between poles s and t. The terminals of the secondary side are either between u and ground or between v and ground. Note, that one of the secondary Coupler-poles has to be grounded. See also below at Restrictions. For example:

Node=1=2=3     Node=1=2=0=3

Restrictions

The Coupler has no inductivity and there is no loss. Further there is no mutual-inductance as in the Transformer-model. Because of the latter you can not connect Couplers in any arbitrary way in ABEC. There are some restrictions in connecting multiple Coupler-elements directly. There are no restrictions with other elements.

  • The poles of the primary side of different Couplers can be connected together, in series or in parallel.
  • One of the secondary poles has to be grounded.
  • A secondary pole must not be directly connected with any other pole of a Coupler and
    should not be connected with the driving-point of the network (pole with the lowest number).

Above circuits demonstrate correct wiring of multiple Couplers. If you remove R2 in the right schematic, you would see an error message.

Example 1

The left above circuit creates a symmetric output signal. At node 12 is the non-inverted signal and at node 13 you will measure the inverted signal.

For the sake of this example a runtime formula is used. The Ratio parameter is not only frequency dependent, but also complex. 'f' is the abscissa frequency.

  Def_Driving
    Value=1

  System  'S1'
    Coupler   'Cp1'
      Node=10=11=12=0
      Ratio={1/(1 + jf/500)}

    Resistor 'R1'
      Node=12   R=10ohm

    Coupler   'Cp2'
      Node=11=0=0=13
      Ratio={1/(1 + jf/500)}

    Resistor 'R2'
      Node=13   R=10ohm
