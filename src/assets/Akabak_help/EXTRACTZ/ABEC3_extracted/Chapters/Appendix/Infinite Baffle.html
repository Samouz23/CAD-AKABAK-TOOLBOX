Mathematically, boundary conditions of the IB are satisfied by using a special type of Green-function for the Helmholtz Integral. This special Green-function happens to yield

vn = 0

for all points at the surface of the Infinite Baffle. In this way there is no need to model the infinite plane (... well, at least approximately ...).

The workings of the trick with the special Green-function can be explained by imagining a mirrored source behind the Infinite Baffle. At receiving point P1 the sound pressure is the sum of the original source and its mirrored image. However, in the plane of the IB the normal velocity yields zero everywhere.

After solving and with the mesh in view, we can display the mirror images with the help of check-box "IB Mirrors".


Concave Shaped Boundaries

The trick with the special Green-function works fine, as long all elements are in front of the Infinite Baffle. However, as soon as there are elements behind the Infinite Baffle then the mirror-technique runs into difficulties.



ABEC provides a solution for this special case, i.e. if there are elements behind the Infinite Baffle. In this case you would need to divide the structure into sub-domains in such a way that the exterior structure exhibits only elements in front of the Infinite Baffle.


An example-simulation may demonstrate that results are different for without and with using the interface-technique:

The above picture shows a cone-shaped diaphragm in an Infinite Baffle. The transparent gasket forms the interface, ie a virtual boundary with continuity of pressure and velocity. The Infinite Baffle is at the level of the cone. The interface couples two sub-domains, one with ElType=Interior and one with ElType=Exterior. The interior sub-domain is bounded by the diaphragm and by the interface. The exterior side of the interface is convex and becomes mirrored in order to satisfy boundary conditions on the Infinite Baffle. If you check Views/Structure/IB Mirrors then ABEC displays also the mirrored elements (last picture above).

The spectra of the simulations display different results. The correct model is on the right, which employs interfaces. The radiation impedance is more pronounced as it should be for a concave shape. Also visible is a slight amplification in the sound pressure curve exactly in the frequency band where the real part of the radiation impedance has its main-peak. Note, that this simulation was not coupled to the mechanical domain, otherwise this amplification can be even more pronounced.
