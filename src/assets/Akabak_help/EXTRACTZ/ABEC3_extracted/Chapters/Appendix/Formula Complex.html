At certain places formulae can be entered in complex form, for example at runtime formula for lumped elements, such as Filter or Impedance.

Imaginary unit

The imaginary unit is identified by the letter j, with

j·j = -1.

If j comes before a number (for example between the minus sign and the value) or before the name of a constant or function or before a bracket, this is equal to multiplying the following value by the imaginary unit. The syntax is thus similar to that of a minus sign. For example:

    h = -jSin(j - -j5);  k = -j(5 + j^(Sin(3)) + h

Results

The Formula-system of ABEC always calculates in complex mode. However, most of the time results are likely to be real. Results can be complex if you specify the imaginary unit or provide out of range arguments (for example √(-5)). Or, if imported data are complex valued.
