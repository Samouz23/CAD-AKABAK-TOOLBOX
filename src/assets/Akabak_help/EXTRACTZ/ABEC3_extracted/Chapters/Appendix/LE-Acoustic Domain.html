In the acoustical domain of the Lumped Element Analysis the potential is the sound-pressure p and the flow is the volume-velocity V. The acoustic power for sinoidal signals is

Pa  =  1/2·p·V'

with pressure p and volume-velocity V peak-values. V’ means conjugate complex.

For an equivalent circuit where the potentials are Voltage, Force and Pressure the acoustic mass is analogue to an electrical inductance and to a mechanic mass (Coil and MechMass). The acoustic potential-storage is the compliance, which has its analogue in the electrical domain by the Capacitor and in the mechanical domain by the MechCompliance.

For modeling complicated lumped-element-circuits we recommend Bauers’ [10-13] method.


Note, that the acoustical domain has the particularity that the sound pressure is not an absolute value but a pressure-variation to the absolute atmospheric pressure. Because of this any element, which represents potential storage, should be grounded. Such an element is the AcouCompliance. However, this concerns also the Duct, Waveguide and Enclosure elements. Because in these components all ports are always grounded they are sketched as two-poles for convenience. Internally these components are automatically expanded to a four-pole.


The volume-velocity is an acoustical measure, which describes the normal velocity of a lump of air-particles through a control-area, S:

V  =  v·S

The term “volume-velocity” stems from the unit of V, which is [V] = m2·m/s = m3/s. The particle-velocity should not be confused with the Speed of Sound c.


A plane acoustic wave in free-space exhibits the relation

p/v  =  ρ·c  =  Z0

with sound pressure p, air-particle velocity v, air-density ρ, Speed of Sound c and specific acoustical radiation impedance Z0.

The time convention, exp(jωt), used by the harmonic analysis is such, that a positive imaginary value of an acoustic impedance symbolizes an acoustical mass, and a negative value indicates a compliance.
