In the axis-symmetric case there are only two indices of points, P1 and P2, with P = (Axial, Radial)T. These points mark the edges of concentric rings. These rings generate the surface-area of truncated cone sections.

A cone-section can be non-truncated if the radius of P1 or P2 is zero, can form a ring if the axial-values are identical, or a cone-section can form a cylinder if the radial-values are identical. For example:

  Elements "Cone"
    RefNodes="Nodes"
    SubDomain=1
    101   10  20    // Dust cap center
    102   20  30    // Dust cap outer
    103   30  40    // Cone first inner section
    104   40  50    // Cone and so on
    ...

The normal of a cone-section points inwards, i.e. towards the Axial-axis, if Δa > 0. For example, Nr is negative for the inner cone-sections in the above side-view diagram on the left. For the last ring Nr is positive, i.e. its normal points outwards.

The order of the specification of nodes controls the principal direction of the normal, which should point always into the domain. The domain is the volume enclosed by the cone-sections. For interior problems the normals should point into the volume enclosed (ElType=BE_Interior), in exterior radiation conditions the normals point away from the body (ElType=BE_Exterior) as explained above.
