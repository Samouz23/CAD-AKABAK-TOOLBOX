The Laplace transfer-function maps an input to an output voltage:


U2 = H(s)·U1



bi and ai are real valued coefficients.

s is the complex-valued Laplace variable. The coefficients can be scaled in a way that a normalized frequency variable can be used, see at f0=.

m and n are the polynomial degrees. n is the order of the transfer-function. Because of accuracy of computation the maximum degree is limited here to m=30 and n=30.

t0 is a constant time delay in [t0] = s.


The denominator hosts the Characteristic Polynom, which zeros are the eigen-frequencies of the filter. In order to be realizable all a's should be positive, non-zero and real valued. The Characteristic Polynom tells us what a network can do potentially.

The numerator polynomial describes where we drive and where we observe a network. The b-coefficients should be real but can take any value otherwise, positive, negative and zero. Depending on the b's we get a low-pass, high-pass or anything in between.
