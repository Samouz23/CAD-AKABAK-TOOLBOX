Radiation Conditions

Radiation Conditions is not a component or so, which you have to implement but rather something we need to decide on if we investigate radiation into open domains, which in ABEC is specified by ElType=Exterior.

The advantage of the Boundary Element method is that it copes well with radiation into exterior domains. We simply define the geometry and the Helmholtz Integral returns the field. In BEM there is no need to distinction near- and far-field - all is included.

However, we need to decide on the Radiation Conditions. Is the radiation into full-space or is an Infinite Baffle better? What kind of approximations are allowed and so on?


Take a horn as an example. After modeling carefully the interior of the horn, its throat, its flaring and at the mouth, we want to observe the directivity. Well, there we are. How does the horn radiates?

In this example, we can decide to place the horn into an infinite baffle, which is always a good starting point, because the Infinite Baffle "cuts away" the outside boundaries of the horn, and in this way we do not need to model it.

Alternatively, we can choose to model as is. In this case we would need to model also the outside skin of the horn, its driver, its flange etc. The result is a response into full-space.

Between the two extremes, there may be many approximations. For example, we can argue that the front radiation is more important than the directivity on the rear and that there is not much reflection to be expected by the details of the outer skin of the horn. In this case we may decide to replace the details of the outer side of the horn by a simple box.


In any case it is important to note that each sub-domain of the Boundary Element Method needs a body of non-zero thickness. If you specify just one plane, or a structure, which is wide open, then this is regarded as a sheet in BEM and would need to be handled in a special way. Calculation of sheets is currently under development.

An example of a sheet is a thin reflecting plane somewhere. It is tempting to model only one side. But you can try it out yourself, you would get different results between the model which gives a thickness to the reflector and the one which ignores it. The thickness should not be too small in order to avoid number noise.
