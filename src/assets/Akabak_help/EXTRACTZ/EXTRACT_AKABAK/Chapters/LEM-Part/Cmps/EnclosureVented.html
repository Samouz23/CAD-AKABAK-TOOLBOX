Enclosure Vented

Home LE-Part Acoustic

The Enclosure Vented-component can be based on lumped elements or use a more complex rectangular enclosure with standing waves. In the world of lumped elements the Enclosure is specified as a two-pole, where one pole is always grounded.
This component features a vent in order to create a Helmholtz-Resonator. Internally the vent is modeled with the help of a Duct element. On one side the vent is connected to the cavity of the enclosure. The aperture at its other side is loaded by a self and mutual radiation impedance. Further there is radiation from the vent. The Radiator elements are added automatically.

There are available several models for the vented enclosure. Also it is possible to either couple the vent-aperture to the BE-Part, or to use an approximation for the radiation impedance of the vent.

Helmholtz Resonator

If you click to edit Vb, Len, fb or Vent Aperture the Helmholtz-Form will open. This input-form features also a little calculator to evaluate the resonance or other parameters of the vented enclosure.

Ref to P-Source

Ref to P-Source is necessary only in Direct Sound mode (see discussion above at Vent Aperture). It allows the vent to radiate. In BEM-mode this reference would be ignored.
Ref to P-Source means a link to a Pressure Point or Pressure Box.

Enclosure Type

(1) Basic lumped elements + duct
(2) Two ducts parallel (deep)
(3) Two ducts in series (long)
Lumped Elements

In this basic model the cabinet exhibits no standing waves. Only standing waves in the vent are taken into account. The cabinet is modeled with the help of lumped elements is a similar way as at the component Enclosure Sealed.

Cab = Vb/(ρ·c2) Rab = ρ·c/(η·Vb)
Parallel Ducts

In this case the model for the vented enclosure consists of two ducts wired in parallel. The first duct stands for the cabinet. We have zero velocity at its rear wall. In this configuration standing waves for the cabinet and for the vent are taken into account. This configuration assumes approximately the same sound pressure at the part of the cavity which is close to the speaker and to the vent.

Series Ducts

This case also models both, the cavity and the vent, with the help of ducts. Only, here the vent is assumed to be distant to the speaker. We assume different sound pressure values at the vent and at the speaker. Hence the two ducts are wired in series.

etab

Etab specifies enclosure losses in the same way as explained at component Duct.

etav and Visc

Etav and Visc specify losses in the vent in the same way as explained at component Duct.
