Membrane is a four-pole-component featuring an mechano-acoustical transducer with a simple mechanical resonator. There are two versions of this component. Membrane (mob) provides coupling to a mobility mechanical network. Membrane (imp) provides coupling to the dual, which is the mechanical network in impedance notation.

The port on the right hand side is driving the acoustic domain. The acoustic port is similar to component DynDriver. One terminal stands for the front side of the diaphragm and the other terminal for the rear. In practice this means that we connect to the upper terminal any acoustic component which shares the sound pressure at the front of the diaphragm. To the bottom terminal we connect acoustic components which have the same pressure at the rear.

Mobility Notation

The port to the left is of the mechanical mobility domain where the potential is the velocity and flow is the force. The component provides already a mass, a spring and a dashpot which is typical for a so-called passive loudspeaker. This kind of loudspeaker is used to extend the transmission band at low frequencies. It works similar to a vented-enclosure. The passive membrane replaces the vent. One can use a normal woofer with leaving the electrical terminals unconnected. Alternatively, one can find dedicated components. In most cases one would simply leave the mechanical terminals unconnected. Note, that one mechanical terminal is naturally connected to ground.

Example 1: LEM Model

To the right we see an example featuring one active woofer and two passive membranes. There are no further connections necessary at the mechanical ports. This is so because no additional forces are acting on the mechanical system of the membrane component. The front sides of the membranes are connected to Radiator components, which in turn are coupled to the BEM-part of the model. The rear of the membranes are pointing inside the enclosure. Here they face a pressure field. At very low frequency where the wave-length is long compared to the dimensions of the cabinet, one could approximate the effect of the volume by a simple acoustic compliance or Enclosure component. However, this example demonstrates a technique where the enclosure is modeled with the help of a concatenation of Duct components. This trick models part of the distributed field inside of the enclosure.

Example 2: BEM Model

If the interior is also modeled with the help of BEM then a passive membrane may become decoupled logistically. The passive membrane front side is coupled to the exterior domain. The associate component is the Radiator. This is exactly the same as with the LEM model. However, using BEM also for the interior there becomes necessary a second Radiator component which couples the interior of the enclosure. After establishing all links between LEM and BEM and entering parameters of the membranes we will find out that the calculation has ignored the membranes. Why, because the membranes are not wired directly of indirectly to the Source-component. Any component not connected to the source gets ignored. Exactly for this case there is provided a simple means, which keeps the Membrane components connected. Provide an extra ground-wire between the orphan parts and the auxiliary ground of the Source. An example is shown in the schematic to the right.

Impedance Notation

The Membrane component is also available for the Impedance Notation of the mechanical domain. The port to the left of Membrane (imp) is of the mechanical impedance domain where the potential is the force and flow is the velocity. The Impedance Notation is dual to the Mobility Notation.

In the Impedance Notation the resonator is modeled with the help of a series connection of the mass, spring and the dashpot. In order to make a simple passive membrane we have to complete the mechanical circuit by a short-cut to ground as shown in the example to the right.

Also for Membrane (imp) holds that we have to make sure it is wired directly or indirectly to the Source-component (see above).
