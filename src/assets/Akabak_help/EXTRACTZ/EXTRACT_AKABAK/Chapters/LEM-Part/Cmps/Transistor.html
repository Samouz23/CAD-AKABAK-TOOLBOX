The Transistor element implements a small signal, broad band model of a transistor. Pole s is the Base, pole t the Collector and pole u the Emitter of the transistor.

Note, all parameters are assumed of small-signal, small variations about the operation point. The operating point is set by the DC-circuit of the transistor. Note also, that in AKABAK we only calculate the frequency dependent circuit. In this scenario the DC-voltage-supply is regarded as short-circuited.



In the schematic shown above after Giacoletto, Y1 is the input-admittance. Y2 is the so-called reverse-transfer-admittance. Often you can neglect Y2 in the audio-frequency-range. Y3 is the output-admittance. g is the forward trans-conductance.

The frequency-dependency of the transistor is simulated with the help of the transition frequency ft of the current-gain h21e = β and the collector-base-capacitance Ccb.

Y1 = 1/h11e + jω·CD with CD = g/(2π·ft)

Y2 = -h12e/h11e + jω·Ccb with Ccb collector-base capacitance

Y3 = h22e = 1/Rce with Rce output resistance

g = h21e/h11e with g forward trans-conductance in Siemens [S]

The internal base-bulk-resistance Rbb is not taken into account here. If the transistor is driven from a low-resistance-source, you may have to insert this resistance externally, so that the frequency characteristic can be correctly reproduced via the capacitance Ccb.

Example: Transistor BC107A in collector-circuit (collector to ground). Operating point: UCE = 5V, IC = 2mA, f = 1kHz
