Duct

Home LE-Part Acoustic

The Duct component creates an acoustic waveguide with constant cross-section in the network. Standing waves along the waveguide are calculated and coupled to other network components according to continuity of pressure and axial volume-velocity. The Duct and Waveguide elements are similar. The difference is that the Duct has a constant cross-section area and the Waveguide has it variable. Further the Duct allows for volume damping.

For convenience, the Duct is specified with only two poles. The two terminals symbolize the two outlets of the Duct. Theoretically, the Duct is always a four-pole. However, because it turns out that the Duct is always grounded (similar to the acoustic compliance) we can specify it conveniently as a two-pole and internally expand the duct to a four-pole.

Cross

Parameter Cross specifies the cross-sectional area. Regarding the cross-section only the fundamental mode is used, and, hence, the shape of the cross-section is ignored. However, for convenience, the input-form provides shape parameters such as the diameter or a reference to a BEM-component. Details are given at chapter Diaphragm Parameters.

Len

Parameter Len specifies the effective length of the Duct. In the lumped element world we often need an end-correction. This end-correction is an additional length which takes into account acoustic radiation. At very low frequencies below fD = c/(π·dD) we may add to the geometric length for each side [16]:
Len' = 0.4250·dD The duct terminates at a large baffle.
Len' = 0.3065·dD The duct terminates freely.

Alternatively the acoustic mass can be read from the observation of the Radiation Impedance with Domain=Acoustic and Calc Mass checked. The end-correction is then Len' = Ma·S/ρ as shown in chapter Radiation Impedance.

Note, that we do not add Len' if the radiation impedance is provided by the BE-Method.

Vf

Parameter Vf specifies the volume of a chamber at the front-side of the Duct.

eta

Parameter eta (η) is a damping factor of a wave traveling along the duct. The ratio of amplitudes are: p2/p1 = exp(-η·d) with distance d between two points of pressure p1 and p2. η is part of the complex wavenumber: k = k' - jη
Large values of η yield strong damping. Typical values are
η Damping
1.0e-5 low
1.0e-3 medium
0.1 high

There is a relation to the acoustic resistance Ra, which can be derived from the duct wave-function of fundamental mode: Za = jρc/SD·tan(k·L) with wave-number k = ω/c. Expanding into Taylor series yields Za = jρc/SD·(k·L + (k·L)3/3 + ...) Taking only the linear term and by substitution of the lossy wavenumber k = k' - jη we can identify the series connection of lumped acoustic resistance and mass, where the acoustic resistance is Ra = L/SD·ρ·c·η with with air-density ρ, speed of sound c.

Note, old Akabaks "Q/f0" parameter is inverse proportional to η. The Qs are more related to second order circuits and coil quality factors, whereas η is home to waveguides. The relation to η is: Q/f0 = 2π/c·1/η  ≈  1/(55·η)

Visc

The Duct-algorithm needs some damping to be present. A default value for the damping is derived from basic viscosity considerations. The formula used is explained at component Acoustic Resistance at chapter Examples - Viscosity and Thermal Exchange. The damping due to viscosity and thermal exchange depends on the perimeter of the duct.
Parameter Visc is simply a multiplier and can be used to magnify or diminish the damping factor due to viscosity:
Visc > 1 Amplification
Visc = 1 Original
Visc < 1 Diminishing
Visc = -1 Switched off

Note, specifying Visc=-1 will cause skipping the viscosity calculation. In this case you must specify parameter eta.

Type

By default the Duct is assumed to have two terminals, one stands for the inlet and one for the outlet. However often we need a Duct where at the outlet there is a wall which is acoustically reflective, this means where the flow is zero (Neumann Condition). In acoustic impedance-networks this means open-loop, hence no connection. With the help of Type = Closed this condition is also graphically emphasized.
