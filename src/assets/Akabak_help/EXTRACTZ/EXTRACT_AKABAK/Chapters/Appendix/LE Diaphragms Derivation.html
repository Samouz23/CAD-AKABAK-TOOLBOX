Lumped Elements are best derived from power considerations. The acoustic power at the diaphragm with area SD is:

Pa = 1/2·∫ p·v dS

with p sound pressure distribution and v velocity distribution (conjugate complex). The integration is performed over the dimensions of the diaphragm. Note, here we assume to know v from specification and p from calculations of the Boundary Element Analysis.

Assuming rigid body motion and forces to be integrated to mean-values over the cross-section, the mechanical power is

Pm = 1/2·F·vm

The acoustic power should be identical to the mechanical power

Pm = Pa

We define the specific field radiation impedance:

zs = p/v

Because p and v are distributions, i.e. vary with position, so depends the value zs on the position. We say it is a field-impedance. However, we can observe that the field-impedance, i.e. the ratio of p and v, at the diaphragm surface is fairly constant in linear acoustics even for vibrating surfaces (in air). This approximation allows us to take it out of the integral:

Pa = 1/2·Zs·∫ v2 dS

with Zs specific lumped radiation impedance.

Combining formula Pa and Pm yields:

Zm·vm2 = Zs·∫ v2 dS

Often the diaphragm is technically arranged in such a way that the mechanical velocity is attached at a single point. For example, a typical loudspeaker has the velocity applied at the voice-coil or at a point in case of a piezo-motor. In this case we can think of v as being the product of a normalized velocity distribution υ and the mechanical lumped velocity vm. This allows us to define an effective diaphragm area SD, which includes the velocity distribution:

Zm = Zs·SD

SD= ∫ υ2 dS

Zs = 2·Pa/(vm2·SD)

The acoustic lumped radiation impedance is then:

Za = Zs/SD

or

Za = Zm/SD2
