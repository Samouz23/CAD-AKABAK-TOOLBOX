The Non-Uniqueness Problem (NUP) is a peculiar mathematical problem of the Boundary Element Method, which occurs only at radiation from bodies in the exterior domain. NUP happens at certain frequencies in the upper band. At such a frequency the solution becomes invalid. Luckily, the invalid frequency range is typically narrow band. For many applications the Non-Uniqueness Phenomenon can be tolerated, often its effects do not show.

AKABAK provides a compensation method, which can be enabled and disabled at global dialog BEM-Parameters.


If there is a body and the BEM solves for the external radiation field then at certain frequencies it fails to solve for the surface potential. It can be shown that these frequencies are identical to the eigen-values of an associated inner-problem yielding zero surface values as well.

Hence the larger the volume of the radiating structure the lower the eigen-frequencies of its inner volume (assuming it is filled with the same medium as in the exterior), and the more critical frequencies we may get in our frequency band of analysis. For example, the radial eigen-frequencies of a sphere are at

k0 = n·π/R

f0 = k0·c/(2π)

with R: radius of sphere, n: number of radial mode, c: speed of sound.

For example for a sphere of R=250mm we have a resonance at n=9, which is f0 = 6174Hz. We can see the effect of this resonance in the BEM-analysis of the exterior radiation problem of a breathing sphere if no compensation is applied. The lower SPL-plot of the same field-point shows no spikes at all, which is of course because here we applied the Dual Surface method.

In AKABAK experiments were done implementing the CHIEF [25], Burton and Miller [26] and the Dual Surface Method [27]. A good compromise of calculation speed, stability and accuracy in the given context provides the Dual Surface Method.
