Direct Sound Analysis calculates the sound pressure ignoring any boundaries. Typically calculation time is short but results can be very approximate. However, in some cases the Direct Sound Analysis is practical, for example, in room or free-field acoustics or in designing loudspeaker cross-over-filters with the help of the MeasRadiator.

The Direct Sound is "embedded" within the BEM-Part. For enabling the Direct Sound mode the only thing you need to do is to make sure that the Subdomain only contains components of type Pressure Source: Pressure Points and Pressure Box. Hence, there should be no components which create acoustic boundaries. The only exception is the Infinite Baffle. There is no solving stage, and you can directly proceed to observations.
For Direct Sound there is no meshing. However, symmetries are taken into account, see chapter menu Global/BEM Parameters.

The Direct Sound-Analysis ignores reflections and diffraction, and, hence, there is no structure to cause such reflections and diffraction effects. The Direct Sound-Analysis calculates the sum-total of the sound pressure at observation points, which is generated by a range of Pressure Sources:

p(r1)  =   Σi G(ri, r1)· pi

G(ri, r1) is the point-source Green-function from point at ri to observation point at r1. p(r1) is the total sound pressure at the observation point at r1. The pi of the above formula includes the weight as given at the Pressure Source multiplied by the value specified at Fixed Driving or by the value, which is calculated by the Lumped Element Network via components MeasRadiator, Radiator, Enclosure (vented) or Speaker.
