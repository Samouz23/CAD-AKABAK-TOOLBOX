Fields (shell) generates regular spaced observation points on a plane, cylinder- or sphere-surface.

Create a new Field-component with the help of the New Component Form. The home of the component is page Observation. Edit an existing component by dbl-click its entry on page Observation or issue menu Edit/Parameters or press CTRL+E.

The result of the Field calculation is the frequency response of the sound pressure. It will be displayed as a contour-plot in the 3D-viewer.

The frequency range is specified at menu Global/Frequencies. After calculation select the frequency or frequency-range to plot at Desktop-Page - Frequencies.

The check-boxes in the list at the side of the desktop lets you show or hide the field. Un-checked Field-records will be skipped at next round of calculation.

Page - General

Provide the name of the component in form of a string.

There is also space for annotations.

Page - Parameter

Analysis Type

Pressure means the type of analysis is set to sound pressure response.

Far-Field

If checked then the simulation is carried out at a large distance. Eventually the amplitude is scaled to specified Radius and the phase to zero-distance.

Far-Field Distance: here you can specify the "large" distance for approximating far-field conditions. If left empty 100 times the size of the radiator is used as far-field distance.

Note, the Far-Field option is only available for the Sphere and the Cylinder.
Note, that the presence of an Infinite Baffle may contradict the Far-Field idea.

View

Planes

View Symmetry yields the output of symmetric fields for planes. The symmetric fields are available only if the model has a symmetric property which can be set at menu Global/BEM Parameters.
If View Symmetry is un-checked then only the original field gets plotted.

Sphere, Cylinder

Only Parts allows to render only part of the sphere or cylinder. Enter numbers where "1" stands for the first octant, "2" for the second and so on. If left blank the whole sphere or cylinder is plotted. In case the model features global symmetry then symmetry is taken into account.

Axis

Select here annotation-options on the shell.

Page - Shell

Plane

Creates a rectangular plane with a regular distribution of observation points.

Width, Height

Size of the plane.

Delta

Sampling distance on the plane. Hence, Delta is the distance between adjoined "microphones".

Direction

Principal orientation of plane. x, y, z are the normal of the plane.

Anchor

Mounting point of plane.

Quads Four Triangles

If checked then quadrilaterals are meshed by four triangles otherwise by two.

Cylinder

Creates an open cylinder and a regular pattern of observation points.

Radius

Distance of observation points from the axis of the cylinder.

Height

Size of cylinder measured along its axis.

Delta Height

Distance of spatial sampling points along its axis.

Delta Phi

Angular sampling distance on perimeter.

Pole

Provides the basic orientation of the cylinder. Select the position of the top-lid of the duct. Further positioning can be specified at Scaling (see below).

Quads Four Triangles

If checked then quadrilaterals are meshed by four triangles otherwise by two.

Sphere

Creates a sphere with a regular pattern of observation points.

Radius

Distance of observation points from origin of sphere.

Delta Theta, Delta Phi

Angular sampling distance on sphere. These values are nominal and depend on the selection of Mesh. If Mesh=Polar... these angles are exact otherwise approximate.

Mesh

The surface of the sphere or cylinder is tesselated into sub-planes. At the vertices of these sub-planes there placed observation points (microphones).
Polar Lines creates a regular angular grid of circles passing through the poles (longitudes). Orthogonal to it there is a regular angular grid of latitude circles. Delta Theta controls the angular spacing of the latitude circles. Delta Phi controls the angular spacing of the longitude circles.
Polar Planes creates a regular angular grid of latitude circles controlled by Delta Theta. On it are distributed planes of approximately same size, which is derived from Delta Phi at the equator.
Cube creates a mesh on a cube, which is then projected on the sphere. The number of planes are: N = 6·sqr(360/(4·Δ) - 1) with Δ = 1/2·(Δθ + Δφ)
Octaeder creates triangles based on recursive bifurcation. The edge length of the triangles are smaller than 1.6·Δ·Radius

Pole

Provides the basic orientation of the sphere. Select the position of the north-pole of the sphere. Further positioning can be specified at Scaling on Page Parameter (see below).

Quads Four Triangles

If checked then quadrilaterals are meshed by four triangles otherwise by two.

Scaling Position

Opens the Scaling-Form where direction and position can be specified. Mounting point is the center of the sphere or the center mid point of the cylinder.

Page - Range

On this page you find controls for the range of output as the ranging directly controls the contouring. Also sources can be selected which shall take part in the simulation. More see chapter Frame - Range.
