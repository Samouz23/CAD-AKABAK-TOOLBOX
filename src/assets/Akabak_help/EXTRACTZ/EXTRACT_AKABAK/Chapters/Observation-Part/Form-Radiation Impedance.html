Form - Radiation Impedance

Home Observation

Radiation Impedance is an observation of the lumped acoustic impedance of a vibrating boundary area.

Create a new Radiation Impedance-component with the help of the New Component Form at page Observation. For editing dbl-click its entry on page Observation, press CTRL+E or issue menu Edit/Parameters

The result of the spectrum calculation is a frequency response which will be displayed with the help of VACS.

The frequency range is specified at menu Global/Frequencies.

Page - Parameters

Driving Boundaries

The vibrating boundaries are assumed specified with the help of a Diaphragm-component or via the Boundary Conditions of a component of the BEM-Part.

These components can be selected in tables Ref 1 and Ref 2.

Often we want to observe the Self Radiation Impedance, which displays the effect of the sound pressure on itself. In this case leave table Ref 2 empty.

The mutual radiation impedance shows the power-coupling between two vibrating groups of boundaries. In this case specify also Ref 2.

Domain

The lumped acoustic impedance is the ratio of the "mean-value" of sound pressure and the volume velocity: Za = p/q. The mean-value is calculated across the effective radiation area:

with normalized velocity distribution ν (normalized means here to the velocity at the driving point of the membrane).

Specific Normalized: It is practical to view the curve normalized to ρ·c and to the effective radiation area S', so that the asymptotic value of the real part of Zn becomes one at high frequencies: Zn = Za·S'/(ρ·c)

Specific radiation impedance is defined as Zs = p/v. In general p and v can be field values of the sound pressure and acoustic velocity. However, if we form the mean-value over S' then we have the lumped version of the specific radiation impedance. Often this integration means a simple multiplication of the acoustic impedance by the area: Zs = Za·S'. The asymptotic value of the real part at high frequencies is ρ·c.

Acoustic is the lumped acoustic radiation impedance Za = p/q with p and v lumped sound pressure and volume-velocity.

Mechanic is the equivalent mechanic radiation impedance Zm = F/v = Za·S'2 with force F acting on the membrane and mechanic velocity v.

Calc Mass

If checked the impedance is divided by jω which yields the associate mass. This option can be helpful to determine the mechanic equivalent mass load of a diaphragm. Or, it can be used to find a value for the end-correction of a waveguide.

Type of Parameter

Impedance displays the radiation impedance, for example Za = p/q.

Admittance displays the radiation admittance, which is Y = 1/Z.
